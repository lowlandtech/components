## Outcome

The repository's own browser host publishes trimmed without a trim warning that names a
component type it renders, so the showcase pages no longer hide a hazard the published
packages do not have.

## The measurement

`dotnet publish src/app/LowlandTech.Components.App.csproj -c Release -p:PublishTrimmed=true`
reports two unique `IL2111` for `Microsoft.AspNetCore.Components.DynamicComponent.Type.set`,
in the generated `Pages/AdminComponents_razor.g.cs` and `Pages/Showcode_razor.g.cs` of
`src/stories`. Both come from the same source shape:

```razor
<DynamicComponent Type="@State.Current.Type" />
```

`StoryMetadata.Type` carries no `DynamicallyAccessedMembers` declaration, so the trimmer
cannot prove that the story component's members survive.

## What changes

| Surface | Change |
|---|---|
| `StoryMetadata.Type` | declares `DynamicallyAccessedMembers(All)`, the requirement `DynamicComponent.Type` asks of its source |
| `StoryRegistry.Register(Type)` | its parameter declares the same requirement |
| `StoryRegistry.RegisterAllStories` | the scan of a loaded assembly is the single reflective root and suppresses the two flow warnings it now produces |

### What the change had to be

Annotating the source of the type alone did not clear the warning: the trimmer reports the
*reflective parameter assignment* of the renderer, not the origin of the value. Two things
were needed and both are recorded above:

- the metadata declares what `DynamicComponent.Type` requires — `StoryMetadata.Type` and
  `StoryRegistry.Register` carry `DynamicallyAccessedMembers(All)`, and the single scan root
  `RegisterAllStories` suppresses the two flow warnings that declaration produces (`IL2072`
  and `IL2062`, the analyzer's and the linker's codes for the same flow);
- the two generated page classes declare the render assignment itself
  (`IL2111` and `IL2110` through `@attribute` on `AdminComponents.razor` and
  `Showcode.razor`), the same shape the blocks components use.

A republish proved the second part is what removes the warning; the first part keeps the
analyzer silent and states the contract the renderer needs.
